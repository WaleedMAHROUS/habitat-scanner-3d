HABITAT SCANNER 3D

Welcome aboard.

Every flight begins with a promise: that everyone on board will return home safely. Wildlife strikes are among the quieter threats to that promise, and the land surrounding an aerodrome does much to shape the risk.

In the next few minutes, you will fly over real satellite imagery to an airport of your choice and survey its surroundings, identifying the water, waste facilities, farmland and green spaces that draw birds and other wildlife toward the runway.

This work is dedicated to raising safety awareness and to strengthening aviation safety by managing wildlife at aerodromes to the highest level of safety that is reasonably practicable.

2WHAT IT IS AND WHAT IT IS NOT
Habitat Scanner 3D is a professional-grade aerodrome wildlife strike intelligence platform designed for aviation safety practitioners, wildlife hazard management teams, aerodrome operators, and safety regulators worldwide.
By entering any ICAO airport code or GPS coordinates, the platform performs a full 360° geospatial habitat reconnaissance within a 20 km radius of the Aerodrome Reference Point (ARP). The virtual drone flies cinematically to the target aerodrome, orbits the ARP, and automatically overlays all surrounding land-use habitats and drainage networks, live-queried at scan time from OpenStreetMap, alongside the world's major migratory bird corridors shown as fixed reference geography (see Migratory Flyways below).
✓ IT IS
  • A fast, consistent first look at the habitats and attractants around any aerodrome in the world.
  • A geographic basis for planning field work and for the Wildlife Hazard Management Programme (WHMP).
  • Transparent: every feature shows its class, distance, source and verification status.
  • Editable: you can add what the scan missed and correct what it got wrong.
✗ IT IS NOT
  • A certified survey or a Wildlife Hazard Assessment.
  • A measurement of bird numbers, species, movements or strike probability.
  • Complete: it can only show what OpenStreetMap and the satellite data contain.
  • A substitute for the judgement of a qualified wildlife hazard specialist.
This is a screening tool, not a certified survey. Automated results are only as complete as current OpenStreetMap coverage in a given area, which varies substantially by region and is measurably lower in parts of the Middle East, Africa, and other areas than in Europe or North America. A blank area on the map means no feature is currently mapped there, not that no attractant exists. Every scan shows a data-confidence indicator, every automated feature is labelled with its verification status and OSM source, and users can manually flag attractants the automated scan missed. Automated output should be field-verified before it is relied on for an operational decision or a regulatory submission.

    A virtual reconnaissance drone navigates from global altitude, descends through the approach sector, and locks onto the ARP in a controlled orbit.
  3. 3

    Habitat polygons, waterway networks, radius rings, and migratory flyways are fetched live from OpenStreetMap Overpass API and rendered in 3D.
  4. 4

    After one full orbit, unlock free 3D exploration: pan, zoom, tilt, and rotate the satellite view to inspect any area in detail.
  5. 5

  6. 6

Hover flyways for species, peak migration windows, and risk level.
  7. 7
    Verify & fill gaps
    Check the data-confidence indicator in the airport panel. In areas with sparse OpenStreetMap coverage, use Add Feature to record a real attractant you can identify from imagery or a site visit; it is exported alongside automated features but clearly marked as manually added.
4ASSESSMENT RADIUS RINGS (ICAO DOC 9137)
13 km Inner Ring (solid red): The radius around the aerodrome reference point that ICAO guidance, including Doc 9137 (Airport Services Manual, Part 3, Wildlife Control and Reduction), uses for assessing wildlife attractants. Habitats and land uses inside this ring are the ones an aerodrome operator is expected to identify, assess and manage.
20 km Outer Ring (dashed red): The extended reconnaissance zone used to identify external attractant pressure sources (e.g. landfills, reservoirs, wetlands, fish farms) that may draw high-risk species onto approach and departure corridors even when located outside the primary management boundary.

5HABITAT CLASSIFICATION & RISK SIGNIFICANCE
Each mapped feature is assigned to one class. The coloured label shows the documented attractant level of the class (VERY HIGH, HIGH, MODERATE or LOW), taken from FAA AC 150/5200-33B and ICAO Doc 9137. The level is then combined with the zone to give the risk tier (section 6). Built-up land (residential, commercial, industrial) is not fetched; any water or waste inside it is mapped as its own class.
WATER BODIES & SHORELINE
Water VERY HIGHBeach / shoreline HIGH
Critical habitat for waterfowl, wading birds, gulls and herons. Open water on approach paths is one of the highest strike-risk habitat types because of mass movement at dawn and dusk. Beaches and shorelines concentrate gulls and shorebirds.
WETLANDS & MARSHES
Wetland VERY HIGH
Natural or artificial marshes are named attractants that guidance recommends siting away from aerodromes. They hold waterfowl, waders and raptors throughout the year.
LANDFILL / WASTE SITES
Waste VERY HIGH
The single highest-risk land-use type near any aerodrome. Active waste sites draw thousands of gulls, corvids, starlings and raptors daily. ICAO uses 13 km from the reference point as its assessment distance.
WASTEWATER TREATMENT
Wastewater VERY HIGH
Treatment works with settling lagoons are highly attractive to gulls, herons, waders and swallows all year, and are often an underestimated strike-risk source near major airports.
FARMS & LIVESTOCK
Farmyard / livestock HIGHAquaculture / fishery HIGH
Poultry farms, feedlots and livestock enclosures attract corvids (especially rooks and jackdaws), raptors and starlings. Fish farms and fishery ponds attract herons, cormorants, ospreys and gulls, even when small.
AGRICULTURAL & OPEN VEGETATION
Cropland HIGHOrchard, pasture, grassland MODERATEScrub, heath LOW
Feeding grounds for flocks of starlings, lapwings, rooks and pigeons. Ploughed or harvested fields draw corvids and finches in numbers. Open grassland supports rodents and insects that attract raptors.
FOREST & WOODLAND
Forest MODERATE
Nesting, roosting and perching habitat for raptors, corvids and many passerines. Dense woodland beside runways raises the chance of raptor strikes.
PARKS, NATURE RESERVES & GOLF
Nature reserve HIGHGolf course, park MODERATE
Managed green spaces and protected areas can hold large bird populations, and are persistent attractants that may be legally difficult to change. Golf turf and water hazards attract geese, gulls and starlings.
MOWN GRASS (LAWNS, VERGES, AIRFIELD)
Mown grass LOW
Short turf has modest value on its own but can hold loafing gulls, geese and starlings. It is drawn only from 1 ha upward so road verges do not clutter the map.
SAND & BARE GROUND
Bare ground LOW
Little food or cover, so a weak attractant on its own. It is mapped for completeness and rated low.
─ Drainage / Waterways (blue lines): Drains, ditches, canals, streams, and rivers mapped as linear features. These corridors attract wading birds and waterfowl and act as movement highways for birds through and across the aerodrome perimeter. Even narrow drainage channels on the airfield itself represent a strike risk in wet conditions.

7HOW THE MAP IS BUILT, STEP BY STEP
This section states exactly what the scanner does to the data, so a specialist can judge what the map can and cannot support. Items marked app-defined are screening choices made for this tool; they are not published standards and are not claimed to be.
  1. DATA IN
  2. 1
    Fetch
    Download the mapped features from OpenStreetMap in six separate parts.
    Full explanation
    The scan asks OpenStreetMap (via the Overpass service) for six separate query parts. Critical attractants (open water, wetlands, waste, wastewater, fish farms, livestock and poultry sites, waterways, runways) are fetched across the full 20 km. Generic cover (forest and woods, grass and meadow, farmland, orchards, scrub, heath, beach and sand, cemeteries, recreation grounds, parks and gardens) is fetched only within 13 km. Built-up land (residential, commercial, industrial) is not fetched. If any part fails, the scan is marked INCOMPLETE and says which part is missing; the result is never presented as complete.
  3. 2
    Classify
    Give each object one habitat class from its tags.
    Full explanation
    Each OpenStreetMap object is assigned one habitat class from its tags (for example natural=water to Water Body, landuse=landfill to Landfill / Waste Site, landuse=grass to Mown Grass, landuse=meadow to Pasture / Meadow). Each class carries an attractant tier: very high, high, moderate or low, with the source cited in the click panel (FAA AC 150/5200-33C, ICAO Doc 9137 and related guidance).
  4. 3
    Remove false points
    Drop dumpsters and recycling bins that are not landfills.
    Full explanation
    Dumpsters and recycling bins are not landfills. They are left out unless a mapped outline of 1 ha or more exists. Each scan reports how many were left out. Multi-part features that come back without an outline are not drawn as boxes; they are listed in the diagnostics.
  5. MEASURE AND FILTER
  6. 4
    Distance and zone
    Measure from the reference point and place each feature in a zone.
    Full explanation
    Distance is measured from the reference point to the nearest edge of the feature (to the centre for point-only features; to the nearest shore for large water). Zones: Area A 0 to 3 km, Area B 3 to 8 km, Area C 8 to 13 km (the ICAO assessment radius), and an extended 13 to 20 km buffer. The A, B, C split follows the Australian NASF Guideline C; the 20 km buffer is app-defined.
  7. 5
    Minimum mapping unit app-defined
    Hide very small features on screen; keep them in counts, report and KML.
    Full explanation
    Features smaller than a threshold are not drawn by default, to keep the map readable and fast, but they are kept for the report and KML and are flagged there. Water, wetland, waste, wastewater, farms and fish farms: 0.05 ha in Area A, 0.25 ha in B, 1 ha in C and beyond. All other classes: 0.25 ha in A, 1 ha in B, 4 ha in C and beyond. Mown grass: never below 1 ha. A switch in the legend shows the hidden features. Because they are hidden only on the map, a small pond can appear in a report and not on screen.
  8. DRAW
  9. 6
    Overlaps app-defined
    Draw one answer per place: the more significant class wins.
    Full explanation
    OpenStreetMap often stacks polygons (a pond inside a park, a landfill inside industrial land). For drawing, the more significant class wins the overlapping area, in this order: waste, wastewater, fish farms, livestock sites; then open water; wetland; farmland and orchards; golf courses; grass, meadow, scrub, heath, beach, bare ground; forest. Parks and nature reserves are drawn as dashed outlines only and never hide anything. Areas, distances, scores and the KML always use the true, untrimmed footprints.
  10. 7
    Water cross-check
    Compare with satellite water; never delete OpenStreetMap features with it.
    Full explanation
    Where available, ESA WorldCover satellite water (10 m pixels, via Google Earth Engine) is drawn faintly as a cross-check. Its outlines are generalised and can be oversized, so it is labelled satellite-derived, is never used to delete an OpenStreetMap feature, and is not used in the combined-attractant test. Water larger than 3 km2 inside the circle is shown as a thin shoreline with a callout, not a filled polygon.
  11. SCORE AND REPORT
  12. 8
    Score
    Attractant level times zone weight gives the risk tier.
    Full explanation
    Each feature: attractant severity weight (1 to 4) multiplied by zone weight (1 to 4). A score of 12 or more is CRITICAL HOTSPOT, 8 to 11 HIGH CONCERN, 4 to 7 MODERATE, below 4 MONITOR ONLY. This is a rule-based screening score, not a statistical model and not a measured strike probability. Size and species-specific factors are not in the score.
  13. 9
    Combined attractants app-defined
    Flag different strong attractants lying within 1 km of each other.
    Full explanation
    Two or more different very-high or high classes within 1 km of each other, all inside 13 km, are flagged as a cluster (FAA AC 150/5200-33C, 2.10: separately acceptable land uses can together form a wildlife corridor). The flag adds information; it never changes a feature score.
  14. 10
    Report, kml and batch
    Package the results together with their data-quality checks.
    Full explanation
    The report and KML carry the scan time, the OpenStreetMap retrieval time, data-confidence checks, counts of features retrieved and below the mapping unit, overlaps resolved, containers left out, and the combined clusters. Batch mode applies the same scan to a list of airports and keeps only a summary per airport; its counts are a screening summary, not a risk score.
What this map cannot tell you: OpenStreetMap is community-maintained. Coverage is thinner in parts of Africa, the Middle East and Asia than in Europe and North America, and an unmapped landfill, farm or pond is simply absent. The scanner cannot see seasonal flooding, active feeding, bird counts or species. Treat every result as a guide for field verification and for the aerodrome's own wildlife hazard assessment, not as a substitute for either.

10SPECIAL CASES AND DESIGN DECISIONS
These notes explain choices that affect what you see. Open any item to read it.
Display rules
generic cover (forest, grass, farmland, parks) is fetched only within 13 km; critical attractants (open water, wetland, waste, wastewater, aquaculture, livestock, golf, reserves) are fetched to 20 km. Features smaller than an app-defined minimum size for their distance are hidden by default (still in report and KML; toggle in the legend). Large water labels appear only for named water within 13 km (max 6). Runway sectors are off by default. Full methodology text follows in a later version.
Many airports at once
scans are queued two at a time, overlap resolution runs one airport at a time in the background, and finishing one airport no longer redraws the others. Habitat polygons are drawn only when zoomed in to about 7 or closer; the rings and pins stay visible at any zoom. Large numbers of airports in one session still use a lot of memory in the browser; for dozens of airports, scan and export them in batches.
Batch mode
paste a list of ICAO codes and the app scans them one at a time, with pauses so the public OpenStreetMap servers are not overloaded, and builds a summary table (very-high attractant counts by distance zone, high, moderate and low counts, nearest very-high attractant, data status). Only a summary is kept per airport, so memory stays flat. The table downloads as CSV. OPEN loads one airport on the map in full detail. Incomplete scans are retried automatically and marked INCOMPLETE if they still fail. Counts are a screening summary, not a risk score.
Combined attractants
FAA AC 150/5200-33C (2.10) notes that land uses that are acceptable on their own can together form a wildlife corridor. The app flags a cluster when two or more different higher-severity classes (very high or high tier) lie within 1 km of each other, edge to edge, inside the 13 km zone. This 1 km rule is an app-defined screening choice, not a published standard. Small features below the minimum mapping unit, park and nature-reserve overlays, and satellite-derived water polygons are not tested. The flag appears in the click panel, the report (Section 3) and the KML, and never changes a single feature score. Reports and KML also state how many features are below the minimum mapping unit.
Mown grass is not pasture
OpenStreetMap landuse=grass (lawns, road verges, interchange infields, airfield grass) is now its own class, "Mown / Amenity Grass", rated LOW and ranked just below pasture. It has its own legend switch and is only drawn from 1 ha upward (an app-defined size limit), so road verges and small infields do not clutter the map; larger areas such as airfield grass and big lawns remain. Only landuse=meadow is called "Pasture / Meadow". Polygons are draped on the ground, so grass beneath a flyover or bridge is drawn where it really lies, which can look as if it sits on the road.
Waste containers are not landfills
dumpsters, skips and recycling bins (OpenStreetMap amenity=waste_disposal, and amenity=recycling that is not a recycling centre) are street furniture, so they are not mapped as waste sites. Landfills, waste transfer stations, recycling centres and any tagged waste compound with a mapped outline of 1 ha or more are kept. Each scan reports how many containers were left out. Open dumpsters at terminals and catering areas can still attract birds; they are a matter for the aerodrome's own site inspection, not this map.
One answer per place
where habitat polygons overlap, only one is drawn. Ranking, highest first: (1) landfill and waste sites, wastewater treatment, aquaculture, farmyards and livestock; (2) open water; (3) wetland; (4) farmland and orchards; (5) golf courses; (6) grassland, pasture, scrub, heath, beach and bare ground; (7) forest; satellite-derived water ranks last and only fills places OpenStreetMap leaves unmapped. Within one rank the smaller polygon wins. Parks and nature reserves are designations, not ground cover, so they appear as dashed outlines and do not hide anything. The ranking is this app's own rule for wildlife-hazard screening (it follows the fixed-priority idea used in OpenStreetMap-to-land-cover conversion and the hazard emphasis of FAA AC 150/5200-33C), not a published standard. Only the drawing is trimmed: areas, distances, scores and the KML keep each feature's full footprint.
Very large lakes
some areas contain lakes so large (for example Lake Nasser near Aswan) that the public OpenStreetMap servers cannot always deliver their detailed outlines. In that case the app asks for a lighter version of the water query (all ponds, lakes and rivers drawn as single outlines) and draws the very large lakes from satellite data (ESA WorldCover, 10 m resolution, outlines approximate). The panel then shows a blue note, “Large lakes shown from satellite data”. Every other habitat class is complete. Satellite polygons are always marked as such and never replace an OpenStreetMap feature.
Long scans
a notice appears only if the data is still not ready after 20 seconds (and never during the flight). Areas with many water bodies or features take longer; please keep the page open.
Ranking rule
features smaller than the minimum mapping unit remain in the counts and the KML export but are not ranked as hotspots. The batch table lists satellite water in its own column, marked “satellite”, and keeps it out of the tier counts.
Management summary
the report starts with a one-page visual summary: headline verdict, key figures, features by zone and risk tier, largest habitat classes, the top five sites, key observations and suggested follow-up for specialist review. The detailed sections follow.
Screen layout
the side panel and the tool buttons can each be hidden with the small panel icons, and the text size control (A+ / A−) goes from 90% to 125%.
Scan integrity
each scan runs six small OpenStreetMap queries instead of one large one, and states in the data-confidence panel whether all six were retrieved. Multi-part OpenStreetMap features (lakes, reservoirs, wetlands, parks, reserves) are now requested with their full outlines; any that still arrive without an outline are left off the map and counted, never drawn as boxes. Straight outline segments of 3 to 4 km or more in water polygons (typically where incomplete OSM data was closed with a straight line) are flagged, and are left out of the shoreline drawn for large water bodies. The thresholds are app-defined heuristics, not a standard.
11MIGRATORY FLYWAYS
The platform overlays the world’s major bird migration corridors, each colour-coded by risk level (High / Medium / Low). Hover any flyway line to see the peak migration windows, key species groups, estimated annual bird volume, and the specific aerodromes most affected. These corridors are fixed reference geography for planning context, not a live or per-flight verified feed — there is no free, continuously updated global tracking dataset at this scale. Treat them as indicative of known seasonal risk, not as real-time bird activity.
Flyways are particularly important for understanding seasonal strike risk spikes — an aerodrome that is low-risk in summer may become extremely high-risk during spring and autumn migration periods when billions of birds are in transit across major corridors.

13FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
How do I start?
Type a four-letter ICAO code in the box (for example HECA) and press INITIATE HABITAT SCAN. If the airport is not in the database, use ADD AIRPORT or enter coordinates instead. The scan usually takes from several seconds to about a minute, depending on how busy the servers are and how much is mapped around the airport.
What do the 13 km and 20 km rings mean?
They follow the assessment distances of ICAO Doc 9137 (Airport Services Manual, Part 3): attractants matter most close to the aerodrome and less with distance. The report also splits the area into zones by distance from the ARP so you can see how many features lie in each.
Where does the data come from, and how accurate is it?
Land use and facilities come from OpenStreetMap (live), water from ESA WorldCover satellite data where needed, and airports from OurAirports. OpenStreetMap is edited by volunteers, so completeness differs between countries and even between districts. A missing feature on the map does not prove it is absent on the ground, and a mapped one may be outdated. Always verify on site.
Why is a known lake, farm or landfill missing?
Either it is not mapped in OpenStreetMap, or it is mapped with a different tag than the scan recognises. Use ADD FEATURE to add it yourself, and consider adding it to OpenStreetMap so everyone benefits.
What do the risk tiers (Critical, High, Moderate, Monitor) mean?
They are screening tiers combining how attractive the feature type is to birds or other wildlife with how close it is to the ARP. They tell you where to look first. They are not a measured strike risk and they do not replace a wildlife hazard assessment.
What is a combined-attractant cluster?
Different attractant classes lying within 1 km of each other, for example a waste site next to a pond. Combined attractants can draw more birds than each one alone, so the report lists them together.
What does "satellite, approx." mean on a feature?
Its outline comes from 10 m satellite land-cover data rather than a hand-drawn map. The outline is approximate and may include artefacts, so check it against the imagery.
The report says the scan is INCOMPLETE. What should I do?
Part of the OpenStreetMap download failed or timed out (the report names which part). Run the scan again, ideally at a quieter time, and compare the counts. Do not use an incomplete scan as a final result.
How do I open the export in Google Earth?
Download the KML or KMZ, then open it in Google Earth (double-click the file, or use File, Open). Features are grouped in folders by class and carry name, class, distance and source in their description.
How do I get the report as a PDF or Word file?
Press GENERATE REPORT. In the report window choose the print or save-as-PDF button (in the print dialog choose Save as PDF, A4, Margins Default, untick Headers and footers, tick Background graphics; a short checklist appears before the dialog opens). For an editable file press EXPORT WORD (.DOCX). The file name starts with the date, then the report name and the airport.
Can I use the result in a regulatory submission?
Use it as a starting point and a map for planning the field work. The regulator will expect verified data and the assessment of a qualified specialist. The report says this on its own pages too.
Does it cost anything, and is my data uploaded?
It uses free and open data sources. Pins, added features and imports stay in your browser page; closing the page discards them unless you exported them.
How do I report a problem or suggest an improvement?
Use the contact link at the end of this page. Include the ICAO code and what you expected to see.
